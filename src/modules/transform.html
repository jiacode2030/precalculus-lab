Transformations of functions

Shift, stretch and flip any graph with four numbers, and track what happens to every point.

The idea

Start from a graph y = f(x) and build a new one: g(x) = a·f(b(x − h)) + k. Each number does one job. h shifts right by h (left if negative). k shifts up by k. a stretches vertically by |a| and flips the graph over the x-axis when a is negative. b compresses horizontally by a factor of 1/|b| and flips over the y-axis when b is negative.

Follow a point. If (x, y) is on the graph of f, then (x/b + h, a·y + k) is on the graph of g. The horizontal changes act on x and look backwards (b makes the graph narrower, h = 2 moves it right); the vertical changes act on y and look natural. To apply them in order: horizontal stretch or flip by b, then shift by h, then vertical stretch or flip by a, then shift by k.

Transformations pass properties on. Zeros of g are at x/b + h for each zero x of f. The range of g is a·(range of f) + k, reversed if a is negative. Even and odd symmetry, intercepts and asymptotes all move with the graph. And any two graphs of the same shape at different sizes and places are related this way: the tide at one harbor and the tide at another.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose the four-point graph. Set a = −1. What does each key point do?

    What you should see

    The graph flips over the x-axis: each point (x, y) goes to (x, −y).

  2. Set b = 2 (a = 1). Does the graph get wider or narrower?

    What you should see

    Narrower: every x-coordinate is halved. A bigger b squeezes the graph, a trap for beginners.

  3. Set h = 2. Is that left or right? Which numbers change?

    What you should see

    Right: g(x) = f(x − 2) needs x one bigger to do what f did, so each point moves 2 to the right. Only the x-coordinates change.

  4. Choose 1/x and set k = 2. What happens to the horizontal asymptote?

    What you should see

    It moves up with the graph, from y = 0 to y = 2. Asymptotes are transformed like everything else.

  5. In the second lab choose the first example and drag the stage slider from 0 to 4. At which stage does the graph flip over?

    What you should see

    At stage 3, the vertical stretch by a = −2: it stretches by 2 and flips. The shift by k = 1 in stage 4 then lifts it, but does not change its shape.

Worked example: moving key points

The graph of f passes through (−2, 1), (0, 3), (2, −1) and (4, 0). Find the images of these points on the graph of g(x) = −2f(2(x − 1)) + 1.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Read the numbers. a = −2, b = 2, h = 1, k = 1.
  2. Horizontal. x → x/b + h = x/2 + 1. The x-values −2, 0, 2, 4 become 0, 1, 2, 3.
  3. Vertical. y → a·y + k = −2y + 1. The y-values 1, 3, −1, 0 become −1, −5, 3, 1.
  4. Answer. The graph of g passes through (0, −1), (1, −5), (2, 3) and (3, 1). It is flipped and stretched vertically, squeezed horizontally and moved right 1 and up 1.

Worked example 2: tides at two harbors

At harbor A, the tide height is h_A(t) meters, with a high tide of 7 m at t = 0 and a low of 1 m at t = 6 hours. At harbor B the same tide arrives 2 hours later and is 1.5 times as high. Write h_B in terms of h_A, and find its high and low.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Later. A delay of 2 hours is a shift right by 2: replace t by t − 2.
  2. Higher. 1.5 times as high is a vertical stretch by 1.5: multiply the whole thing.
  3. Formula. h_B(t) = 1.5·h_A(t − 2).
  4. Key points. High tide: (0, 7) → (2, 1.5 · 7) = (2, 10.5). Low tide: (6, 1) → (8, 1.5 · 1) = (8, 1.5). The range goes from [1, 7] to [1.5, 10.5].

Common mistakes

  • Moving the wrong way for h: g(x) = f(x − 2) moves RIGHT.
  • Thinking b = 2 widens the graph. It narrows it by a factor of 2.
  • Doing the shifts before the stretches: f(2x − 2) is a shift by 1 after the squeeze, not by 2.
  • Forgetting that a vertical shift k also moves asymptotes and the range.

Why the horizontal changes look backwards

For g(x) = f(2x), the output at x is what f produced at 2x, so g reaches f's value at x = 1 where f reached it at x = 2. The graph gets there twice as fast, so it is squeezed. In the same way g(x) = f(x − 2) at x = 5 gives f(3): g repeats f's outputs two units later, to the right.
