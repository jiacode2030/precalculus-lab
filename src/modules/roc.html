Change in tandem & rates of change

Two quantities that change together tell a story. The average rate of change measures how fast one changes compared with the other.

The idea

A function pairs each input with an output, so when the input changes, the output changes in tandem. As x increases, f is increasing if its outputs go up, and decreasing if they go down. How it goes up matters too: if the output rises faster and faster, the graph is concave up; if it rises more and more slowly, concave down.

The average rate of change of f on [a, b] is the change in output divided by the change in input: (f(b) − f(a)) / (b − a). It is the slope of the secant line through (a, f(a)) and (b, f(b)), and it has units: output units per input unit, such as cm per second.

The rate of change at a point is what the average rates approach as the interval around that point shrinks. You can estimate it with a small interval. A positive rate means increasing; a negative rate means decreasing; a rate that is itself increasing means concave up.

  1. Choose Cylinder and press Play. How does the height graph look, and why?

    What you should see

    A straight line: every second adds the same volume to the same-width column, so the height rises by the same amount each second. A constant rate of change.

  2. Choose Cone, point down. When is the water rising fastest?

    What you should see

    At the start, where the glass is narrowest. As it widens, the same volume makes a thinner layer: the height keeps increasing, but at a decreasing rate. The graph is concave down.

  3. Choose Round flask and Play. Where does the graph switch from concave down to concave up?

    What you should see

    At the widest part of the ball, halfway up. Below it the glass widens (rate falling, concave down); above it the glass narrows (rate rising, concave up). That switch is a point of inflection.

  4. In the second lab choose Cyclist with a = 2, b = 4, then press Shrink. What number does the average rate approach?

    What you should see

    From 5 m/s on [2, 4] down toward 4 m/s: the rate at t = 2, the cyclist's speed at that instant.

  5. Choose Cooling coffee. Is the average rate positive or negative? What does it mean?

    What you should see

    Negative, in degrees per minute: the temperature is decreasing. The rates get closer to 0 later on: it cools more and more slowly (decreasing, concave up).

  1. Formula. On [a, b]: (h(b) − h(a)) / (b − a), in cm per second.
  2. [0, 10]. (12.41 − 0)/10 ≈ 1.24 cm/s.
  3. The rest. (15.63 − 12.41)/10 ≈ 0.32, (17.89 − 15.63)/10 ≈ 0.23, (19.69 − 17.89)/10 ≈ 0.18 cm/s.
  4. Describe it. All the rates are positive, so h is increasing. They get smaller, so it rises more and more slowly: the graph is concave down. That fits the glass: it gets wider as it fills.

Worked example 2: speed from position

A cyclist starts from a light and has gone d(t) = 0.5t² + 2t meters after t seconds. Find the average speed on [2, 4], [2, 3] and [2, 2.1], and estimate the speed at t = 2.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Values. d(2) = 2 + 4 = 6, d(4) = 8 + 8 = 16, d(3) = 4.5 + 6 = 10.5, d(2.1) = 2.205 + 4.2 = 6.405.
  2. [2, 4]. (16 − 6)/2 = 5 m/s.
  3. [2, 3]. (10.5 − 6)/1 = 4.5 m/s. [2, 2.1]. (6.405 − 6)/0.1 = 4.05 m/s.
  4. Estimate. As the interval shrinks, the average rates approach 4 m/s: the cyclist's speed at t = 2 (what a speedometer would show).

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by the change in output instead of the change in input.
  • Leaving off units: an average rate of change is "output units per input unit".
  • Confusing decreasing with concave down: a graph can increase and be concave down at the same time.
  • Thinking one average rate describes the whole interval: on [0, 40] the glass averages 0.49 cm/s, but it was much faster at the start.

Why the secant slope is the average

If you replaced the actual change by steady change at the average rate, you would end at exactly the same place: f(a) + (average rate)(b − a) = f(b). That straight-line trip is the secant line. A car that drives 150 km in 2 hours has an average rate of 75 km/h, even if it stopped for lunch.
