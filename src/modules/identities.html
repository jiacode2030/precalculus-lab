Identities & equivalent forms

The same function can wear many different formulas. Identities let you swap one for another to simplify, evaluate or solve.

The idea

An identity is an equation that is true for every input where both sides are defined. Graphically, the two sides have exactly the same graph. The most important one is the unit circle itself: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Divide it by cos²θ or sin²θ and you get 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ and 1 + cot²θ = csc²θ.

The sum and difference formulas give the trig values of combined angles: sin(α ± β) = sin α cos β ± cos α sin β and cos(α ± β) = cos α cos β ∓ sin α sin β. Note that sin(α + β) is not sin α + sin β. Setting β = α gives the double-angle formulas: sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ and cos 2θ = cos²θ − sin²θ = 1 − 2 sin²θ = 2 cos²θ − 1.

Identities are tools. Use them to find exact values (sin 75° = sin(45° + 30°)), to rewrite an expression into one trig function before solving an equation, and to turn a product or a square into a sum, which is easier to graph and interpret.

Same graph, different name: 10 identities

Two expressions are identical when their graphs match everywhere. Predict, then see the match.

1. Which expression is identical to 1 − cos²θ?

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, so 1 − cos²θ = sin²θ.

2. Which expression is identical to 2 sin θ cos θ?

The double-angle formula: sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ. The graph has period π.

3. Which expression is identical to cos²θ − sin²θ?

cos 2θ = cos²θ − sin²θ. (The Pythagorean identity would give 1 only for cos² + sin².)

4. Which expression is identical to sin(θ + π/2)?

Shifting sine left by π/2 gives cosine: sin(θ + π/2) = sin θ cos(π/2) + cos θ sin(π/2) = cos θ.

5. Which expression is identical to cos(θ − π)?

cos(θ − π) = cos θ cos π + sin θ sin π = −cos θ. Half a turn flips the point to the opposite side.

6. Which expression is identical to tan θ cos θ?

tan θ cos θ = (sin θ / cos θ) cos θ = sin θ, wherever cos θ ≠ 0 (the graph has holes there).

7. Which expression is identical to 1 + cot²θ?

Divide sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by sin²θ: 1 + cot²θ = csc²θ.

8. Which expression is identical to cos(π/2 − θ)?

Cofunction identity: cos(π/2 − θ) = sin θ. The "co" in cosine means "sine of the complement".

9. Which expression is identical to sin 2θ / (2 cos θ)?

sin 2θ / (2 cos θ) = 2 sin θ cos θ / (2 cos θ) = sin θ (with holes where cos θ = 0).

10. Which expression is identical to sec²θ − tan²θ?

From 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ: sec²θ − tan²θ = 1, a flat line with holes at the asymptotes of tan.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose sin² + cos² and slide θ. Does the orange line ever leave the blue curve?

    What you should see

    Never: the left side is always exactly 1, a flat line. Every point (cos θ, sin θ) is on the circle of radius 1.

  2. Choose sin 2θ. Compare 2 sin θ cos θ with 2 sin θ at θ = 1.

    What you should see

    sin 2 ≈ 0.909 and 2 sin 1 cos 1 ≈ 0.909 agree; 2 sin 1 ≈ 1.683 does not. Doubling the angle is not doubling the sine.

  3. Choose sin(θ + β) vs sin θ + sin β. Set β = 0.79 (about π/4). Are the curves the same?

    What you should see

    No: sin θ + sin β reaches 1.71 but sin(θ + β) never exceeds 1. Sine does not distribute over addition: sin(α + β) ≠ sin α + sin β.

  4. In the second lab choose Squares on the legs. Drag θ. What do the two square areas add up to?

    What you should see

    Always 1, the area of the square on the hypotenuse (the radius). That is the Pythagorean theorem, and it is sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

  5. Choose Adding two angles with α = 0.79 and β = 0.52 (about π/4 and π/6). Compare the two ways of computing sin(α + β).

    What you should see

    Directly, sin(1.31) ≈ 0.966. By the formula, sin α cos β + cos α sin β ≈ 0.707(0.866) + 0.707(0.5) ≈ 0.966. That is sin 75° = (√6 + √2)/4.

Worked example

sin θ = 3/5 and θ is in quadrant II. Find cos θ, tan θ, sin 2θ and cos 2θ exactly.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Picture. Height 3/5 on the unit circle, on the left side (quadrant II).
  2. Pythagorean identity. cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ = 1 − 9/25 = 16/25, so cos θ = ±4/5. In quadrant II cosine is negative: cos θ = −4/5.
  3. Tangent. tan θ = (3/5)/(−4/5) = −3/4.
  4. Double angle. sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ = 2(3/5)(−4/5) = −24/25.
  5. And cos 2θ = cos²θ − sin²θ = 16/25 − 9/25 = 7/25. Check: (−24/25)² + (7/25)² = (576 + 49)/625 = 1 ✓.

Worked example 2: power from a wall outlet

A space heater element on a US outlet draws power p(t) = 340 sin²(120πt) watts, t in seconds. Use an identity to rewrite p(t) as a sinusoid. What is the average power, and how often does the power repeat?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. The problem. A squared sine is not in a sinusoid form a cos(bt) + d, so its midline and period are hard to read.
  2. Identity. From cos 2x = 1 − 2 sin²x: sin²x = (1 − cos 2x)/2.
  3. Rewrite. p(t) = 340 · (1 − cos(240πt))/2 = 170 − 170 cos(240πt). Now it is a sinusoid with midline 170 and amplitude 170.
  4. Read it. The average (midline) is 170 W, half the peak. The period is 2π/(240π) = 1/120 s: the power pulses 120 times a second, twice per voltage cycle, because it is the same whether the current flows one way or the other.

Common mistakes

  • Writing sin(α + β) = sin α + sin β, or sin 2θ = 2 sin θ.
  • Choosing the wrong sign after a square root: use the quadrant to decide.
  • Confusing sin²θ (the square of sin θ) with sin(θ²).
  • Dividing both sides of an equation by sin θ and losing the solutions where sin θ = 0. Factor instead.
  • Using the wrong form of cos 2θ: pick the one that matches what you know (1 − 2 sin²θ when you know sin θ).

Solving with identities

To solve cos 2θ = cos θ on [0, 2π), rewrite with one function: 2cos²θ − 1 = cos θ, so 2cos²θ − cos θ − 1 = 0, which factors as (2 cos θ + 1)(cos θ − 1) = 0. Then cos θ = −1/2 (θ = 2π/3, 4π/3) or cos θ = 1 (θ = 0). The identity turned a double-angle equation into a quadratic in cos θ.
