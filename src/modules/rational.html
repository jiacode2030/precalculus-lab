Rational functions

A ratio of polynomials can blow up where the denominator is zero and settle down far away. Its zeros and asymptotes give the whole skeleton of the graph.

The idea

A rational function is a ratio of two polynomials, f(x) = p(x)/q(x). It is not defined where q(x) = 0. The zeros of f are the zeros of p, as long as q is not also 0 there. At each zero of q that is not cancelled, the graph has a vertical asymptote: the outputs grow without bound, positive or negative, as x approaches it.

Far away, only the leading terms matter, as with polynomials. If deg p < deg q the outputs shrink toward 0: horizontal asymptote y = 0. If deg p = deg q they approach the ratio of leading coefficients: y = aₙ/bₙ. If deg p > deg q there is no horizontal asymptote: f keeps growing (when the degrees differ by exactly 1 it follows a slanted line, found by division).

At a vertical asymptote x = c, look at the sign of f on each side: it depends on the sign of the factors and on whether the factor (x − c) appears an odd or even number of times. An odd power: the two sides go opposite ways (one to +∞, one to −∞). An even power: both sides go the same way. Write it as limx→c⁺ f(x) = ∞.

Far away: 8 rational functions

Compare the degrees of the top and the bottom, predict the far-away behavior, then see it.

1. What happens to f(x) = 2x / (x² + 1) far to the left and right?

The numerator has the smaller degree, so the bottom wins far away: the outputs shrink to 0.

2. What happens to f(x) = (3x² − 1) / (x² + 2) far to the left and right?

The degrees are equal, so the ratio of the leading coefficients is the horizontal asymptote.

3. What happens to f(x) = (x² + 1) / (x − 1) far to the left and right?

The numerator has the bigger degree, so the outputs keep growing: no horizontal asymptote. Dividing gives the slanted line it follows.

4. What happens to f(x) = (−2x + 5) / (x − 4) far to the left and right?

The degrees are equal, so the ratio of the leading coefficients is the horizontal asymptote.

5. What happens to f(x) = 4 / (x² + 1) far to the left and right?

The numerator has the smaller degree, so the bottom wins far away: the outputs shrink to 0.

6. What happens to f(x) = x³ / (x² − 4) far to the left and right?

The numerator has the bigger degree, so the outputs keep growing: no horizontal asymptote. Dividing gives the slanted line it follows.

7. What happens to f(x) = (5x² + x) / (2x² − 3) far to the left and right?

The degrees are equal, so the ratio of the leading coefficients is the horizontal asymptote.

8. What happens to f(x) = (x + 1) / (x² + x + 2) far to the left and right?

Worked example: reading a rational function

For f(x) = (x − 1)(x + 2) / ((x − 3)(x + 1)), find the zeros, the vertical asymptotes, the horizontal asymptote, and the sign of f just to the right of x = 3.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Zeros. The numerator is 0 at x = 1 and x = −2, and the denominator is not 0 there: zeros 1 and −2.
  2. Vertical asymptotes. The denominator is 0 at x = 3 and x = −1, and nothing cancels: x = 3 and x = −1.
  3. Horizontal asymptote. Degrees are equal (2 and 2); leading coefficients 1 and 1: y = 1.
  4. Right of x = 3. At x = 4: (3)(6)/((1)(5)) = 3.6 > 0, so f(x) → +∞ as x → 3⁺. At x = 2: (1)(4)/((−1)(3)) = −4/3, so f → −∞ from the left: opposite sides, as expected for an odd power.

Worked example 2: cost per unit

Making n units costs a $500 setup fee plus $8 per unit, so the average cost per unit is A(n) = (500 + 8n)/n. Find A(10), A(50) and A(100), and describe what happens as n gets large.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. A(10) = (500 + 80)/10 = $58.
  2. A(50) = (500 + 400)/50 = $18. A(100) = (500 + 800)/100 = $13.
  3. Large n. Equal degrees (1 and 1), leading coefficients 8 and 1: horizontal asymptote y = 8. A(n) = 8 + 500/n, so the setup fee gets spread over more and more units.
  4. Meaning. The average cost keeps falling toward the $8 per-unit cost but never reaches it. At n → 0⁺ it blows up: a vertical asymptote at n = 0.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking a graph can never cross its horizontal asymptote. It can, in the middle.
  • Cancelling terms instead of factors, like (x + 2)/(x + 3) → 2/3.
  • Writing a vertical asymptote as a y-value. A vertical asymptote is x = c.
  • Forgetting that a zero of the denominator that also cancels is a hole, not an asymptote.

Why equal degrees give the ratio of leading coefficients

Divide the top and the bottom by the highest power of x. Every other term then has x in its denominator and shrinks toward 0, leaving the ratio of the leading coefficients. For (3x² − 5)/(2x² + 7x): divide by x² to get (3 − 5/x²)/(2 + 7/x) → 3/2.
