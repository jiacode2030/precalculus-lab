Rates of change in polar

As θ turns, does the curve move toward the origin or away from it, and how fast? The answer is in r, not in x or y.

The idea

For a polar function r = f(θ), the average rate of change of r on [θ₁, θ₂] is (f(θ₂) − f(θ₁)) / (θ₂ − θ₁): how much the distance to the origin changes per radian of turning, on average. It is the slope of a secant on the (θ, r) graph.

The sign of r and the direction r is changing together decide whether the point is getting closer to or farther from the origin. The distance is |r|. If r is positive and increasing, or negative and decreasing, |r| grows: farther. If r is positive and decreasing, or negative and increasing, |r| shrinks: closer.

Where r changes from increasing to decreasing while positive, the curve is at a local maximum distance from the origin (the tip of a petal, the outer edge of a limaçon).

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. With r = 1 + 2 sin θ, set θ₁ = π/2 and Δθ = 2π/3 (θ₂ = 7π/6). Compute the average rate of change, then read it.

    What you should see

    r goes from 3 to 0, so (0 − 3)/(2π/3) = −9/(2π) ≈ −1.43. r is positive and decreasing the whole way, so the point moves closer to the origin, reaching it at 7π/6.

  2. Now shrink Δθ toward 0.02 while θ₁ stays at π/2. What happens to the average rate?

    What you should see

    It approaches 0: at θ = π/2 the (θ, r) graph is at its peak, so r is momentarily neither increasing nor decreasing. The polar point is at its farthest from the origin.

  3. Choose r = 3 cos(2θ). Set θ₁ = π/4 (0.785) and Δθ = π/4. Is r positive? Is the point getting closer or farther?

    What you should see

    r goes from 0 to −3: negative and decreasing. |r| grows from 0 to 3, so the point moves farther from the origin, tracing a petal on the opposite side.

  4. Choose the sprinkler r = 10 + 4 cos θ. Compare the average rate of change on [0, π/2] and on [π, 3π/2].

    What you should see

    On [0, π/2]: (10 − 14)/(π/2) = −8/π ≈ −2.55 ft per radian (edge coming closer). On [π, 3π/2]: (10 − 6)/(π/2) = +8/π ≈ 2.55 (edge moving away).

Worked example

For r = f(θ) = 1 + 2 sin θ, find the average rate of change of r on [π/2, 7π/6], and say whether the curve is getting closer to or farther from the origin on that interval.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Endpoints. f(π/2) = 1 + 2(1) = 3 and f(7π/6) = 1 + 2(−1/2) = 0.
  2. Interval width. 7π/6 − π/2 = 7π/6 − 3π/6 = 2π/3.
  3. Average rate of change. (0 − 3)/(2π/3) = −9/(2π) ≈ −1.432: r drops about 1.43 units per radian, on average.
  4. Check the sign of r. On this interval sin θ > −1/2 (until 7π/6), so r > 0, and sin θ is decreasing, so r is decreasing.
  5. Conclusion. r is positive and decreasing, so the distance |r| shrinks: the curve gets closer to the origin, reaching it at θ = 7π/6.

Worked example 2: how far the sprinkler reaches

A lawn sprinkler waters a region whose edge is r = 10 + 4 cos θ feet from the sprinkler, θ in radians. (a) Find the average rate of change of r on [0, π/2] and interpret it. (b) Is the edge getting closer or farther on [π, 3π/2]?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Endpoints. r(0) = 10 + 4 = 14 ft; r(π/2) = 10 + 0 = 10 ft.
  2. (a) Rate. (10 − 14)/(π/2 − 0) = −4/(π/2) = −8/π ≈ −2.546 ft per radian.
  3. Interpretation. As the spray direction turns from θ = 0 to θ = π/2, the edge of the watered region gets closer to the sprinkler at an average of about 2.55 feet per radian of turning.
  4. (b) r(π) = 6 ft and r(3π/2) = 10 ft. r is positive and increasing, so the edge is getting farther: average rate (10 − 6)/(π/2) = 8/π ≈ +2.546 ft per radian.

Common mistakes

  • Saying "r is decreasing, so the point gets closer" when r is negative. Then |r| is growing: it moves away.
  • Using the change in x or y instead of the change in r.
  • Forgetting the units: the rate is in units of r per radian.
  • Subtracting in one order on top and the other on the bottom.
  • Using degrees in the denominator: θ₂ − θ₁ must be in radians unless told otherwise.

Why the sign of r matters

The distance from the origin is |r|, not r. When r is negative, the point sits on the opposite ray at distance −r. If r goes from −1 to −3, it is decreasing, but the distance goes from 1 to 3: the point moves away. So always check two things: the sign of r and whether r is increasing or decreasing.
