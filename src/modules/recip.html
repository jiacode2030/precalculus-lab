Secant, cosecant & cotangent

Flip each trig function upside down and you get three more. Small values become huge, and every zero becomes an asymptote.

The idea

The reciprocal trig functions are sec θ = 1/cos θ, csc θ = 1/sin θ and cot θ = 1/tan θ = cos θ / sin θ. Each is undefined exactly where the function it flips is zero, which gives a vertical asymptote.

Flipping a number keeps its sign and swaps big and small: 1/0.5 = 2 and 1/(−0.1) = −10. Since sine and cosine stay between −1 and 1, secant and cosecant are never between −1 and 1: |sec θ| ≥ 1 and |csc θ| ≥ 1. Where the parent touches ±1, the reciprocal touches ±1 too (the U-shaped branches turn around there).

They have lengths on the unit circle: draw the tangent line at (1, 0). The radius extended meets it at height tan θ, and the distance from the origin to that point is |sec θ|. On the horizontal tangent line y = 1 the same trick gives cot θ and |csc θ|.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose sec θ and drag θ to 0. Why is sec 0 = 1, and why is the secant graph at its lowest positive value there?

    What you should see

    cos 0 = 1 is the biggest cosine can be, so its reciprocal 1/1 = 1 is the smallest positive secant. On the circle, the sec segment is just the radius along the x-axis: length 1.

  2. Slide θ toward π/2 (1.571). What happens to cos θ, sec θ and the orange sec segment?

    What you should see

    cos θ shrinks toward 0, so sec θ = 1/cos θ blows up. The radius line meets x = 1 farther and farther up, and at π/2 it never meets it: an asymptote.

  3. Choose csc θ and set θ = 7π/6 (3.665). Predict the sign and value.

    What you should see

    sin(7π/6) = −1/2, so csc(7π/6) = −2. Negative, because the reciprocal keeps the sign.

  4. Choose cot θ. Where are its asymptotes, compared with tan θ?

    What you should see

    cot θ is undefined where sin θ = 0: θ = 0, π, 2π. Those are exactly the zeros of tan θ, and cot θ is zero where tan θ has asymptotes (π/2, 3π/2).

  5. In the second lab, set a = 2. How close to 0 does the reciprocal get now?

    What you should see

    The wave reaches ±2, so the reciprocal reaches ±1/2 and never gets closer to 0. A taller wave gives a flatter reciprocal.

Worked example

Find the exact values of sec(2π/3), csc(2π/3) and cot(2π/3).

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. The point. 2π/3 is in quadrant II with reference angle π/3: (cos, sin) = (−1/2, √3/2).
  2. Secant. sec(2π/3) = 1/cos(2π/3) = 1/(−1/2) = −2.
  3. Cosecant. csc(2π/3) = 1/(√3/2) = 2/√3 = 2√3/3 ≈ 1.155.
  4. Cotangent. cot(2π/3) = cos/sin = (−1/2)/(√3/2) = −1/√3 = −√3/3 ≈ −0.577. Check: tan(2π/3) = −√3, and 1/(−√3) = −√3/3 ✓.

Worked example 2: a lighthouse beam

A lighthouse stands 2 km from a straight shoreline. Its beam makes an angle θ with the line straight out to the shore. (a) Show that the beam travels d = 2 sec θ km to reach the shore and the lit spot is x = 2 tan θ km along the shore. (b) Find d and x at θ = π/3. (c) What happens as θ → π/2?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Right triangle. The side adjacent to θ is the 2 km to the shore. The beam is the hypotenuse d, so cos θ = 2/d and d = 2/cos θ = 2 sec θ.
  2. Along the shore. The opposite side is x, so tan θ = x/2 and x = 2 tan θ.
  3. (b) θ = π/3: sec(π/3) = 1/(1/2) = 2, so d = 4 km; tan(π/3) = √3, so x = 2√3 ≈ 3.46 km.
  4. (c) As θ → π/2, cos θ → 0, so sec θ → ∞: the beam nearly parallels the shore and the spot runs off without bound. θ = π/2 is a vertical asymptote of both d and x.

Common mistakes

  • Writing sec θ = 1/sin θ. The "co" functions pair up: cosecant goes with sine, secant with cosine.
  • Confusing sec θ with arccos θ (cos⁻¹ θ). One is a reciprocal, the other an inverse.
  • Putting the asymptotes of sec θ where sin θ = 0. They are where cos θ = 0.
  • Expecting sec θ = 0 somewhere. A reciprocal is never 0.
  • Forgetting the sign: csc θ is negative wherever sin θ is.

Reading a reciprocal graph from its parent

Sketch the parent lightly. Draw a vertical asymptote at each zero. Where the parent is ±1, plot ±1. Then, between asymptotes, the reciprocal is on the same side of the axis as the parent and gets farther from the axis where the parent gets closer: each hump of the sine wave turns into a U opening away from the axis.
