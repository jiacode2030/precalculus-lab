Vectors

A vector is an arrow: it has a length and a direction, but no fixed start. Adding vectors puts them tip to tail, and the components do the arithmetic.

The idea

A vector is a quantity with magnitude and direction, written in components as ⟨a, b⟩: a units right and b units up. Its magnitude is |v| = √(a² + b²) and its direction is the angle θ with tan θ = b/a (choose the quadrant from the signs of a and b). Going the other way, a vector of magnitude r at angle θ has components ⟨r cos θ, r sin θ⟩. A unit vector has magnitude 1: divide a vector by its length.

To add vectors, add components: ⟨a, b⟩ + ⟨c, d⟩ = ⟨a + c, b + d⟩. Geometrically, place the second arrow at the tip of the first (tip to tail); the sum goes from the start to the end. A scalar multiple kv has components ⟨ka, kb⟩: the same direction (or reversed if k < 0) and length |k||v|. Subtraction u − v = u + (−v). Velocities, forces and displacements add this way, which is why a wind changes a plane's path.

A vector-valued function r(t) = ⟨x(t), y(t)⟩ gives a position vector for each t: it is a parametric function written as a vector. The displacement from t₁ to t₂ is r(t₂) − r(t₁), and the average velocity is that displacement divided by t₂ − t₁, a vector pointing along the secant of the path.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set u = ⟨3, 4⟩ and v = ⟨−2, 1⟩. What is u + v, and how long is it?

    What you should see

    u + v = ⟨1, 5⟩, with length √(1 + 25) = √26 ≈ 5.10. Notice it is not 5 + √5: lengths do not add, components do.

  2. Make v = −u. What is u + v, and what does the picture show?

    What you should see

    The zero vector ⟨0, 0⟩: v undoes u, so the tip returns to the start.

  3. In the second lab set r = 10 and θ = 90°. What are the components?

    What you should see

    ⟨10 cos 90°, 10 sin 90°⟩ = ⟨0, 10⟩: a vector pointing straight up with no horizontal part.

  4. In the third lab choose the circle and set t₁ = 0, t₂ = 3.1. Why is the displacement almost 2r?

    What you should see

    At t = 0 the point is (3, 0) and at t ≈ π it is on the opposite side (−3, 0): displacement ⟨−6, 0⟩ of length about 6 = 2r.

Worked example: add and scale

Let u = ⟨3, 4⟩ and v = ⟨−2, 1⟩. Find |u|, the unit vector in the direction of u, u + v and its magnitude, and 2u − v.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Magnitude. |u| = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5. The unit vector is u/|u| = ⟨3/5, 4/5⟩ = ⟨0.6, 0.8⟩.
  2. Place v at the tip of u (or just add components).
  3. Sum. u + v = ⟨3 + (−2), 4 + 1⟩ = ⟨1, 5⟩, with |u + v| = √(1 + 25) = √26 ≈ 5.10.
  4. Scalar multiple and difference. 2u = ⟨6, 8⟩, so 2u − v = ⟨6 − (−2), 8 − 1⟩ = ⟨8, 7⟩.

Worked example 2: a plane in a crosswind

A plane flies at 250 mph at 30° above east (relative to the air). A wind blows at 40 mph toward the west. Find the plane's actual velocity, speed and direction.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Plane in components. ⟨250 cos 30°, 250 sin 30°⟩ = ⟨216.5, 125⟩.
  2. Wind. Toward the west is ⟨−40, 0⟩.
  3. Add. ⟨216.5 − 40, 125 + 0⟩ = ⟨176.5, 125⟩. The speed is √(176.5² + 125²) = √46777 ≈ 216.3 mph.
  4. Direction. tan θ = 125/176.5 = 0.708, so θ = tan⁻¹ 0.708 ≈ 35.3° above east: the wind slows the plane down and turns it away from its heading.

Common mistakes

  • Adding lengths instead of components: |u + v| is usually not |u| + |v|.
  • Finding the direction with tan⁻¹(b/a) without checking the quadrant: ⟨−3, 4⟩ is not in quadrant IV.
  • Treating the vector as fixed at the origin: a vector can be drawn anywhere, only its length and direction matter.
  • Using degrees and radians on the calculator in mismatch when computing r cos θ.

Dot products and the angle between vectors

The dot product of u = ⟨a, b⟩ and v = ⟨c, d⟩ is a single number: u · v = ac + bd = |u||v| cos θ, where θ is the angle between them. When u · v = 0 the vectors are perpendicular. For example ⟨3, 4⟩ · ⟨4, −3⟩ = 12 − 12 = 0, and they are indeed at a right angle. The dot product is the tool behind "how much of this force acts along that direction".
