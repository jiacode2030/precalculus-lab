End behavior

Far from the origin, a polynomial looks like its leading term. Degree and sign alone decide where the ends go.

The idea

For very large |x|, the highest-power term of a polynomial is so much bigger than the others that it decides everything: the end behavior of f(x) = aₙxⁿ + … + a₀ is the same as that of its leading term aₙxⁿ. The middle can wiggle; the ends cannot.

Two facts settle the ends. Even degree: both ends go the same way, up if aₙ > 0 and down if aₙ < 0. Odd degree: the ends go opposite ways, down on the left and up on the right if aₙ > 0, the reverse if aₙ < 0.

Write it with limits: limx→∞ f(x) = ∞ means f(x) grows without bound as x does. For example, for f(x) = −2x³ + 5x, limx→∞ f(x) = −∞ and limx→−∞ f(x) = ∞. For a factored polynomial, multiply the leading terms of the factors to get the leading term.

Zoom out: 8 end behaviors

Predict the ends from the formula, then zoom out. The wiggles in the middle do not matter.

1. Zoom out on y = x³ − 4x. What do the ends do?

The leading term is x³: degree 3 (odd), coefficient positive. Odd degree: the ends go opposite ways.

2. Zoom out on y = −x⁴ + 3x² + 1. What do the ends do?

The leading term is −x⁴: degree 4 (even), coefficient negative. Even degree: both ends go the same way.

3. Zoom out on y = −x³ + 2x². What do the ends do?

The leading term is −x³: degree 3 (odd), coefficient negative. Odd degree: the ends go opposite ways.

4. Zoom out on y = x⁴ − 5x² + 4. What do the ends do?

The leading term is x⁴: degree 4 (even), coefficient positive. Even degree: both ends go the same way.

5. Zoom out on y = −0.5x⁵ + 4x³ − 2. What do the ends do?

The leading term is −0.5x⁵: degree 5 (odd), coefficient negative. Odd degree: the ends go opposite ways.

6. Zoom out on y = (x − 1)²(x + 1)(x − 3). What do the ends do?

The leading term is x⁴: degree 4 (even), coefficient positive. Even degree: both ends go the same way.

7. Zoom out on y = −(x + 2)(x − 1)². What do the ends do?

The leading term is −x³: degree 3 (odd), coefficient negative. Odd degree: the ends go opposite ways.

8. Zoom out on y = 3 − x². What do the ends do?

The leading term is −x²: degree 2 (even), coefficient negative. Even degree: both ends go the same way.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose x³ − 6x² + 4 and zoom out slowly. When do the two graphs look alike?

    What you should see

    Near the origin they look nothing alike. By a half-width of about 100, the ratio f(x)/x³ at the edge is above 0.9 and the curves are almost on top of each other.

  2. Choose −(x − 3)²(x + 1). What is its leading term without expanding?

    What you should see

    Multiply the leading terms of the factors: −(x²)(x) = −x³. Odd degree, negative: up on the left, down on the right.

  3. In the second lab, keep a > 0 and step n from 1 to 6. What alternates?

    What you should see

    Odd n: down on the left, up on the right. Even n: up on both ends. Only the parity of n and the sign of a matter.

  4. Switch to a < 0. What happens to every picture?

    What you should see

    It flips upside down (a reflection in the x-axis), so every arrow flips.

  5. Set n = 2 and then n = 8. How are the shapes different, and is the end behavior?

    What you should see

    x⁸ is flatter near 0 and steeper outside [−1, 1], but both go up on both ends: same end behavior.

Worked example: end behavior from the formula

Describe the end behavior of f(x) = −2x⁵ + 30x³ + x − 7 with limits, and show that the leading term dominates.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Leading term. −2x⁵: degree 5 (odd), coefficient −2 (negative).
  2. Ends. Odd and negative: limx→∞ f(x) = −∞ and limx→−∞ f(x) = ∞.
  3. Check at x = 10. f(10) = −200,000 + 30,000 + 10 − 7 = −169,997, and −2(10⁵) = −200,000: ratio ≈ 0.85.
  4. At x = 100. The ratio is ≈ 0.9985. The other terms matter less and less: f(x)/(−2x⁵) → 1.

Worked example 2: a model that runs out

A town's population t years after 1990 was modeled by P(t) = −0.1t³ + 3t² + 40t + 500 for 0 ≤ t ≤ 30. What does the model predict in the long run, and when does it predict the town is empty?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. On the data. P(0) = 500, P(10) = 1100, P(20) = 1700, P(30) = 1700: the model fits growth that is leveling off.
  2. End behavior. Leading term −0.1t³: limt→∞ P(t) = −∞. The model must eventually predict a falling, then negative, population.
  3. When P = 0. A graphing calculator gives t ≈ 42.3, around 2032, after a peak of about 1813 near t ≈ 25.
  4. Lesson. A cubic fit is reasonable near its data, but its end behavior is set by its leading term, not by the town. Do not extrapolate far.

Common mistakes

  • Using the constant term or the first term written instead of the highest-power term.
  • Forgetting the sign of the leading coefficient.
  • Thinking the middle wiggles change the end behavior.
  • For a factored form, adding degrees wrongly: (x − 1)²(x + 2)³ has degree 5.

Why the leading term wins

Factor out the leading term: f(x) = aₙxⁿ(1 + (aₙ₋₁/aₙ)/x + … + (a₀/aₙ)/xⁿ). Every term in the parentheses except the 1 has x in a denominator, so it shrinks toward 0 as |x| grows. The parentheses approach 1, and f(x) behaves like aₙxⁿ.
