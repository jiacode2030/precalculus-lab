Parametric rates of change

A moving point has two rates: how fast it moves sideways and how fast it moves up. Their ratio is the slope of the path.

The idea

For a parametric function (x(t), y(t)), each coordinate has its own rate of change. Over an interval [t₁, t₂], the average rates are Δx/Δt = (x(t₂) − x(t₁))/(t₂ − t₁) and Δy/Δt, defined the same way. They are the horizontal and vertical speeds of the point, on average.

The slope of the line through the two points of the path, the average rate of change of y with respect to x, is the ratio of the two: Δy/Δx = (Δy/Δt) / (Δx/Δt), as long as Δx ≠ 0. The time cancels. As the interval shrinks, these rates approach the rates at an instant, and the slope approaches the direction the point is heading.

Signs tell direction: if x(t) is increasing on an interval, the point moves right; decreasing, left. If y(t) is increasing it moves up. Where x(t) has a turning point the path has a vertical tangent (the point momentarily moves straight up or down); where y(t) turns, a horizontal one.

Which way is it moving? 6 cases

Read the direction from x(t) and y(t) alone, then see the path.

1. x(t) and y(t) near the marked time are shown. Which way is the point moving?

At the marked time x(t) is decreasing (moving left) and y(t) is increasing (moving up): the point heads up and left. (x = 2 cos t, y = 2 sin t, t = 1.2)

2. x(t) and y(t) near the marked time are shown. Which way is the point moving?

At the marked time x(t) is decreasing (moving left) and y(t) is decreasing (moving down): the point heads down and left. (x = t² − 2t, y = 3 − t, t = 0.5)

3. x(t) and y(t) near the marked time are shown. Which way is the point moving?

At the marked time x(t) is increasing (moving right) and y(t) is increasing (moving up): the point heads up and right. (x = 1 + t, y = (t − 1)², t = 2.0)

4. x(t) and y(t) near the marked time are shown. Which way is the point moving?

At the marked time x(t) is increasing (moving right) and y(t) is decreasing (moving down): the point heads down and right. (x = 2 sin t, y = sin 2t, t = 5.3)

5. x(t) and y(t) near the marked time are shown. Which way is the point moving?

At the marked time x(t) is decreasing (moving left) and y(t) is decreasing (moving down): the point heads down and left. (x = 3 − t²/2, y = t² − 4t + 3, t = 1.2)

6. x(t) and y(t) near the marked time are shown. Which way is the point moving?

At the marked time x(t) is increasing (moving right) and y(t) is increasing (moving up): the point heads up and right. (x = t − sin t, y = 1 − cos t, t = 2.0)

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose Kicked ball with t₁ = 0 and t₂ = 1. Then move both up by 1 second. Which rate stays the same?

    What you should see

    Δx/Δt = 18 m/s on every interval: x(t) = 18t is linear. Δy/Δt drops from 7.1 to −2.7 m/s: the ball rises, then falls.

  2. Choose Runner on a track, t₁ = 0 and t₂ = 20. Why is Δx/Δt negative while the runner is going forward?

    What you should see

    Running counterclockwise from (50, 0) to (0, 50), x shrinks from 50 to 0: Δx/Δt = −2.5 m/s, moving left, even though the runner never slows down.

  3. Choose Figure-eight and set t₁ = 0, t₂ = π (3.14). What is the slope of the chord, and why?

    What you should see

    Both endpoints are the origin, so Δx = Δy = 0: no chord, the slope is undefined. Average rates of 0 do not mean the point stood still.

  4. In the second lab, choose Circle and shrink h toward 0.01. What does the chord turn into?

    What you should see

    The tangent direction: the chord rotates until it just touches the circle at the point, perpendicular to the radius.

  5. With Figure-eight, find a time where the tangent is vertical. What is x(t) doing there?

    What you should see

    At t = π/2 the point is at the right end (3, 0): x(t) = 3 sin t reaches its maximum and turns around, so Δx/Δt → 0 while y keeps changing. The path is vertical there.

Worked example

A ball is kicked with x(t) = 18t and y(t) = 1 + 12t − 4.9t² (meters, seconds). Find the average rates of change of x and y, and the slope of the path, on [0, 1] and on [1, 2].

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Positions. t = 0: (0, 1). t = 1: (18, 8.1). t = 2: (36, 5.4), since y(2) = 1 + 24 − 19.6 = 5.4.
  2. [0, 1]. Δx/Δt = (18 − 0)/1 = 18 m/s; Δy/Δt = (8.1 − 1)/1 = 7.1 m/s; slope Δy/Δx = 7.1/18 ≈ 0.394.
  3. [1, 2]. Δx/Δt = 18 m/s again; Δy/Δt = (5.4 − 8.1)/1 = −2.7 m/s; slope = −2.7/18 = −0.15.
  4. Read it. Same sideways speed, but the vertical rate went from positive to negative: the ball passed its highest point between t = 1 and t = 2 (at t ≈ 1.22 s).

Worked example 2: a runner on a circular track

A runner goes counterclockwise around a circular track of radius 50 m, centered at the origin, starting at (50, 0) and taking 80 s per lap: x(t) = 50 cos(πt/40), y(t) = 50 sin(πt/40). Find Δx/Δt and Δy/Δt on [0, 20], and compare the distance run with the straight-line distance.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Positions. t = 0: (50, 0). t = 20: πt/40 = π/2, so (0, 50), a quarter lap.
  2. Rates. Δx/Δt = (0 − 50)/20 = −2.5 m/s (moving left); Δy/Δt = (50 − 0)/20 = 2.5 m/s (moving up). The chord has slope 2.5/(−2.5) = −1.
  3. Distance run. A quarter of the circumference: 2π(50)/4 = 25π ≈ 78.54 m, at a constant 78.54/20 ≈ 3.93 m/s.
  4. Straight line. The chord is 50√2 ≈ 70.71 m, so the average rates describe a slower, straight-line trip (√(2.5² + 2.5²) ≈ 3.54 m/s). Average rates only see the endpoints.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing Δx/Δt by Δy/Δt for the slope: it is (Δy/Δt) / (Δx/Δt), rise over run.
  • Reading a negative Δx/Δt as "slowing down": it means moving left.
  • Expecting the slope of the path to exist when Δx = 0, or that zero average rates mean no motion.
  • Using x-values instead of t-values in the denominator of Δx/Δt.

Rates at an instant

Shrinking the interval turns the average rates into the rates at an instant: the horizontal and vertical velocity. In calculus these are x′(t) and y′(t), and the slope of the path is dy/dx = y′(t)/x′(t). Precalculus works with average rates and with the signs (which way, and where it turns); the lab shows the limit as a picture.
