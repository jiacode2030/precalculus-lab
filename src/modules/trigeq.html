Trig equations & inequalities

Solving a trig equation means finding where a wave meets a horizontal line: find every crossing in one period, then add whole periods.

The idea

To solve sin θ = k, isolate the trig function first, then picture the line y = k crossing the wave (or the horizontal line at height k crossing the unit circle). For −1 < k < 1 it crosses twice per period; at k = ±1 it touches once; for |k| > 1 there is no solution.

Find the two angles in [0, 2π) with the reference angle and the quadrant signs. Then the general solution adds whole periods: θ = θ₁ + 2πk and θ = θ₂ + 2πk for sine and cosine, θ = θ₁ + πk for tangent. The inverse function gives one solution; the picture gives the rest.

For an inequality like sin θ > k, the solutions of the equation are the boundaries. Between them, the wave is either above the line or below it: test one point, or just look. With an inside factor, as in sin(2θ) = k, solve for 2θ over the stretched interval [0, 4π) first, then divide.

Where does the line cross? 8 equations

Each equation is a horizontal line meeting a wave. Predict the crossings, then check.

1. Solve sin θ = 1/2 on [0, 2π).

Height 1/2: π/6 in quadrant I and its mirror π − π/6 = 5π/6 in quadrant II, where sine is also positive.

2. Solve cos θ = −√2/2 on [0, 2π).

Width −√2/2: quadrants II and III (cosine negative), reference angle π/4: 3π/4 and 5π/4.

3. Solve tan θ = 1 on [0, 2π).

Tangent repeats every π: π/4 and π/4 + π = 5π/4.

4. Solve sin θ = −1 on [0, 2π).

The line y = −1 just touches the bottom of the wave once per period: θ = 3π/2.

5. Solve 2 cos θ − 1 = 0 on [0, 2π).

Isolate first: cos θ = 1/2. Quadrants I and IV: π/3 and 2π − π/3 = 5π/3.

6. Solve sin(2θ) = 0 on [0, 2π).

sin(2θ) completes two cycles in [0, 2π), so twice as many zeros: 2θ = 0, π, 2π, 3π gives θ = 0, π/2, π, 3π/2.

7. Solve cos θ = 2 on [0, 2π).

The line y = 2 never meets the cosine wave: cosine never exceeds 1. No solution.

8. Solve tan θ = −√3 on [0, 2π).

Slope −√3: reference angle π/3 in quadrants II and IV, where tangent is negative: 2π/3 and 5π/3 (π apart).

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Solve sin θ = 1/2: set k = 0.5. Where are the two solutions on the circle?

    What you should see

    π/6 and 5π/6: the horizontal line at height 1/2 cuts the circle in quadrants I and II, mirror images across the y-axis.

  2. Switch to cos with k = 0.5. Why are the solutions now in quadrants I and IV?

    What you should see

    Cosine is the width, so the line is vertical, x = 1/2. It cuts the circle at π/3 and 5π/3, mirror images across the x-axis.

  3. Choose sin and "f(θ) > k" with k = −0.5. Read the shaded intervals.

    What you should see

    [0, 7π/6) ∪ (11π/6, 2π): everywhere except the bottom stretch of the circle where the height dips below −1/2.

  4. Choose tan with k = 1. How many solutions in [0, 2π), and how far apart?

    What you should see

    Two, π/4 and 5π/4, exactly π apart: the period of tangent. The line y = x through the origin cuts the circle at both.

  5. In the second lab set k = 0.5 and step b from 1 to 4. Count the solutions.

    What you should see

    2, 4, 6, 8: sin(bθ) runs through b full cycles in [0, 2π), and each cycle meets the line twice.

Worked example

Solve 2 sin θ + 1 = 0 on [0, 2π), give the general solution, then solve 2 sin θ + 1 > 0 on [0, 2π).

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Isolate. 2 sin θ = −1, so sin θ = −1/2.
  2. Reference angle and quadrants. |−1/2| = sin(π/6), so the reference angle is π/6. Sine is negative in quadrants III and IV: θ = π + π/6 = 7π/6…
  3. …and θ = 2π − π/6 = 11π/6. General solution: θ = 7π/6 + 2πk or θ = 11π/6 + 2πk, for any integer k.
  4. Inequality. sin θ > −1/2 everywhere the wave is above the line: [0, 7π/6) ∪ (11π/6, 2π). Check θ = 0: 2(0) + 1 = 1 > 0 ✓.

Worked example 2: when can the boat leave?

In a simplified model, the depth of water at a harbor entrance is h(t) = 3 + 1.5 cos(πt/6) meters, t hours after high tide. A boat needs at least 3.75 m of water. During which times in the first 12 hours can it pass?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Inequality. 3 + 1.5 cos(πt/6) ≥ 3.75.
  2. Isolate. 1.5 cos(πt/6) ≥ 0.75, so cos(πt/6) ≥ 1/2. Let u = πt/6; for 0 ≤ t ≤ 12, u runs over [0, 2π].
  3. Boundaries. cos u = 1/2 at u = π/3 and u = 5π/3, so t = 6u/π = 2 and 10 hours.
  4. Which side? cos u ≥ 1/2 near u = 0 (width close to 1): u in [0, π/3] or [5π/3, 2π]. So 0 ≤ t ≤ 2 and 10 ≤ t ≤ 12: the 2 hours on either side of high tide, 4 hours of every 12.

Common mistakes

  • Stopping at the one answer the calculator gives. arcsin(−1/2) = −π/6 is not even in [0, 2π); the two answers are 7π/6 and 11π/6.
  • Dividing by the trig function and losing solutions: in sin θ cos θ = sin θ, factor (sin θ(cos θ − 1) = 0) instead of dividing by sin θ.
  • Forgetting to solve for the inside: for sin(2θ) = 1/2, solve for 2θ on [0, 4π), then divide by 2.
  • Using + 2πk for tangent. Its period is π, so θ = θ₁ + πk.
  • Getting the inequality backwards: check one test point.

Why there are two solutions per period

A horizontal line at height k (with −1 < k < 1) cuts the unit circle in two points, mirror images across the y-axis: θ and π − θ. A vertical line at width k cuts it in two points mirrored across the x-axis: θ and −θ (or 2π − θ). That is the whole reason behind the formulas θ₂ = π − θ₁ for sine and θ₂ = 2π − θ₁ for cosine.
