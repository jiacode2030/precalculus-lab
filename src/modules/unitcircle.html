Sine, cosine & the unit circle

An angle is a rotation. Turn a point around a circle of radius 1: its x-coordinate is the cosine and its y-coordinate is the sine.

The idea

Start at the point (1, 0) and rotate counterclockwise by an angle θ. Wherever you land on the unit circle (radius 1), the point is (cos θ, sin θ). That is the definition: no right triangle needed, so θ can be 125°, −30° or 7π.

Angles are measured in radians: the length of arc the angle cuts on the unit circle. A full turn is 2π (about 6.28) radians, so 180° = π. On a circle of radius r the arc is r times longer: s = rθ.

Every angle has a reference angle: the acute angle to the x-axis. The reference angle sets the sizes of sin and cos; the quadrant sets their signs. Learn quadrant I (0, π/6, π/4, π/3, π/2) and you know the whole circle.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set the angle to 125°. Which coordinate is negative, and why does the tangent come out negative?

    What you should see

    The point is in quadrant II: x = cos 125° ≈ −0.574 is negative and y = sin 125° ≈ 0.819 is positive. tan = sin/cos ≈ −1.428, negative because the signs differ: the radius slopes down to the right.

  2. Turn snapping on and step through 30°, 150°, 210° and 330°. What stays the same, and what changes?

    What you should see

    All four have reference angle 30°, so |sin| = 1/2 and |cos| = √3/2 every time. Only the signs change: (+,+) in I, (−,+) in II, (−,−) in III and (+,−) in IV.

  3. Slide to 90°. What happens to tan θ, and what does the orange tangent line do?

    What you should see

    cos 90° = 0, so tan 90° = 1/0 is undefined. The radius is vertical, and the line through it never meets the vertical line x = 1: the tangent segment runs off to infinity.

  4. Set 30°, then 390°, then −330°. Compare the points.

    What you should see

    All three are the same point (√3/2, 1/2). Angles that differ by a full turn (360° = 2π) are coterminal, which is why sine and cosine repeat with period 2π.

  5. In the second lab, keep θ = 1 and drag the radius from 1 to 3. What happens to the arc length and to y/r?

    What you should see

    The arc length s = rθ triples (from 1 to 3), but y/r stays sin 1 ≈ 0.841. The ratio depends only on the angle, which is why sin θ = y/r works on a circle of any size.

Worked example

A bicycle wheel has radius 0.35 m. The tire valve starts level with the hub on the right (3 o'clock). The wheel turns 150° counterclockwise. (a) Where is the valve relative to the hub? (b) How far did the valve travel along the rim?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Convert the angle. 150° × π/180 = 5π/6 radians. It lands in quadrant II, with reference angle 30°.
  2. Height. On a circle of radius r the point is (r cos θ, r sin θ). y = 0.35 sin(5π/6) = 0.35 × 1/2 = 0.175 m above the hub.
  3. Width. x = 0.35 cos(5π/6) = 0.35 × (−√3/2) ≈ −0.303 m: 0.303 m to the left of the hub (the negative sign means left).
  4. (a) Answer. The valve is at (−0.303, 0.175) m relative to the hub: 0.303 m left and 0.175 m up.
  5. (b) Arc length. s = rθ = 0.35 × 5π/6 ≈ 0.916 m. The angle must be in radians for s = rθ.

Worked example 2: exact values from the reference angle

Find the exact values of sin(4π/3), cos(4π/3) and tan(4π/3).

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Locate the angle. 4π/3 = π + π/3: past the negative x-axis by π/3, so quadrant III.
  2. Reference angle. The acute angle to the x-axis is 4π/3 − π = π/3.
  3. Sizes from quadrant I. sin(π/3) = √3/2 and cos(π/3) = 1/2.
  4. Signs from the quadrant. In quadrant III both coordinates are negative: sin(4π/3) = −√3/2 and cos(4π/3) = −1/2.
  5. Tangent. tan = sin/cos = (−√3/2)/(−1/2) = √3. Positive, because the two negatives cancel (the radius slopes up to the right through the origin).

Common mistakes

  • Mixing degrees and radians: sin(30) on a calculator in radian mode is sin of 30 radians, about −0.988, not 1/2.
  • Using the angle itself instead of the reference angle: sin(5π/6) is sin(π/6), not "sin of something bigger".
  • Getting the signs wrong: x is cosine (width) and y is sine (height). Quadrant II has negative cosine, positive sine.
  • Saying tan 90° = 0. It is undefined, because cos 90° = 0.
  • Using s = rθ with θ in degrees.

Why the circle definition

In a right triangle an angle can only be between 0° and 90°. Periodic things (a wheel, a tide, a sound wave) keep going around, so precalculus redefines sine and cosine as coordinates on the unit circle. For acute angles the two definitions agree: the radius is the hypotenuse (length 1), the height is the opposite side and the width is the adjacent side.

Because the point is on the circle x² + y² = 1, every angle satisfies sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. That one fact is the Pythagorean theorem, and it gives one value from the other: if sin θ = 3/5 in quadrant II, then cos θ = −4/5.
